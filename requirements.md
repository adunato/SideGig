# Side-Revenue Market Research Specification

## Objective

Identify several credible side-revenue opportunities at the intersection of:

1. Demonstrable, existing customer demand.
2. Daniele’s genuine domain expertise.
3. His ability to build highly efficient AI-assisted production systems.
4. A low-maintenance, intermittent operating model.

The research must not assume that something is commercially viable merely because Daniele can produce it, nor recommend unrelated products simply because they sell successfully.

## Relevant expertise

### Primary

- Advanced software development, particularly using AI coding agents.
- Designing agentic workflows that produce substantial output with limited manual effort.
- Strong practical understanding of LLMs, prompting, context construction, evaluation and content-production pipelines.
- Building complex roleplay-chatbot systems, including character behaviour, memory, context management, continuity, model selection and realistic interactions.
- Familiarity with SillyTavern-style ecosystems and custom roleplay implementations.

### Secondary

- Previous experience developing relatively complex games, although not recently and without commercially shipping a title.
- Technical, commercial, management and product-delivery experience.
- Senior telecoms expertise, particularly wholesale solutions, although telecom consulting, expert networks and similar time-for-money activities are specifically unattractive.

## Desired operating model

- Target: approximately a few thousand pounds per year, not replacement income or a scalable startup.
- Work must be optional, intermittent and divisible into small pieces.
- It should be possible to pause activity for weeks without harming customers or the product.
- Low ongoing maintenance, support, customer communication and deadline pressure.
- No continuous publishing schedule or dependency on regular audience engagement.
- Prefer modest, predictable revenue over speculative high upside.
- Prefer an existing marketplace or distribution channel with demonstrated buyer intent.
- Some listing and product-positioning work is acceptable, but the opportunity should not require becoming an SEO, advertising or social-media specialist.
- Low initial expenditure and controlled personal liability.
- Ethical output that provides genuine value rather than automated content pollution.

## Legal and administrative constraints

- Intended to operate in the UK as an individual/sole trader.
- Ideally reported as additional self-employment income through Self Assessment.
- No limited company, company accounts or separate corporate administration.
- No separate business bank account unless a platform strictly requires it.
- A lightweight legal structure may be considered only if genuinely necessary, but not an Ltd.
- Because sole-trader liability is personal, favour low-risk digital products and avoid safety-critical products, regulated advice, sensitive corporate work or software handling important customer data or money.
- Employment-contract, conflict-of-interest and IP implications must be considered, particularly in relation to VMO2.

## Required value-creation model

Daniele’s expertise should normally operate upstream as a production advantage:

**Domain expertise + AI/coding capability → efficient production system → valuable finished product → established buyer market**

The AI workflow itself is not automatically the product.

The value chain must be:

- Long enough to create something customers cannot easily obtain free of charge.
- Short enough to avoid becoming a SaaS company, agency, publishing operation or second job.
- Preferably based on a finished digital product or tightly productised output.
- Capable of benefiting from automation through lower production cost, faster variation, stronger quality control or capabilities unavailable to less technical competitors.
- Located, where possible, in a domain where Daniele can personally assess quality and understand the buyer—not merely automate production in an unfamiliar field.

## Explicitly unattractive models

- Selling generic prompts, AI workflows, agent templates or roleplay-system kits without strong evidence of paid demand.
- Consulting, freelancing, expert networks or other direct exchanges of time for money.
- Telecom consulting or GLG-style calls.
- SaaS products requiring beta users, customer acquisition, subscriptions and continuous operation.
- Standalone web apps requiring an audience, product management and ongoing support.
- SEO-driven blogs, affiliate-content sites or high-volume AI content farms.
- Patreon, newsletters or creator models requiring continuous free content and community engagement.
- Opportunities selected solely because they sell on Etsy or another marketplace when they require unrelated artistic, educational, manufacturing or specialist expertise.
- Products whose apparent opportunity is mostly based on a few exceptional incumbent sellers.

## Research methodology requirements

The work should be treated as a structured research project rather than a single-shot recommendation.

### Stage 1: Market landscape

Identify multiple broad markets where Daniele’s expertise could plausibly contribute both to production and product quality. Do not select a winner at this stage.

### Stage 2: Market validation

For every market, verify:

- That customers pay for finished products in the category.
- Actual sales, paid downloads, marketplace payouts, verified reviews or credible disclosed revenue.
- Whether recent entrants—not only established incumbents—can obtain sales.
- Free substitutes and why customers nevertheless purchase.
- Market saturation, failure cases and likely survivorship bias.
- Whether the required domain expertise genuinely overlaps with Daniele’s.

Listings alone are not evidence of demand. Marketplace-wide revenue proves that a market exists but not that an individual opportunity is viable.

### Stage 3: Specific opportunity scan

Within validated markets, identify several narrowly defined products or production models. Each must explain:

- The buyer and problem being solved.
- The finished product being purchased.
- The existing evidence of demand.
- The domain knowledge required to produce it well.
- How Daniele’s AI/coding capability creates a material advantage.
- The length of the value chain.
- Initial build effort and marginal production effort.
- Ongoing maintenance, support and fulfilment requirements.
- Distribution and marketing requirements.
- Approximate unit economics and sales volume needed for a few thousand pounds annually.
- Legal, IP, privacy, platform-policy and liability risks.
- Ethical considerations.
- Principal uncertainties and the cheapest way to validate them.

## Decision principles

- Market demand and personal capability must both be present; neither can substitute for the other.
- Do not assume that automation compensates for missing domain expertise.
- Do not assume expertise itself is directly sellable.
- Prefer repeatable production advantages over novelty or an easily copied prompt.
- Prefer several plausible, imperfect opportunities over one confidently presented “best idea.”
- Clearly separate verified facts, analytical inference and unresolved uncertainty.
- The final shortlist should result from consistent comparison across opportunities, followed by focused deep dives—not intuition formed during the initial scan.
