# Compass Google Maps Scraper

- **Product / provider:** `compass/crawler-google-places` / Compass
- **Channel:** [Apify Store](../overview.md)
- **Opportunity area:** Lead generation & business intelligence
- **URL:** https://apify.com/compass/crawler-google-places
- **Why selected:** Established market benchmark for business/location data on Apify, with very large current adoption, long operating history, extensive reviews and a mature enrichment surface. It establishes the incumbent-scale economics and capability requirements against which newer lead-generation products can be compared.
- **Assessment date:** 2026-09-15

## 1. Case Overview

Compass Google Maps Scraper is a mature Apify Actor that converts Google Maps search and place data into structured business datasets. It supports keyword/category/location discovery, place-level extraction, reviews and additional enrichment, with output available through datasets, exports, API integrations and schedules.

At assessment date the Store page showed roughly **36K monthly active users**, **602K total users**, **5.1K bookmarks** and a **4.7/5 rating from about 1.8K reviews**. A third-party analysis based on Apify's public API measured approximately **3.77M public runs in 30 days** on 9 September 2026, with a **95.7% success rate**. Even allowing for free-plan and test usage, this is strong evidence that Google Maps business data is a large recurring market rather than an occasional utility.

## 2. Commercial Opportunity

### Product / service

The Actor sells structured access to businesses and places visible through Google Maps. Core output includes business identity, category, address, coordinates, phone, website, rating, review counts, opening hours, prices and other place attributes. The product can also retrieve reviews and enrich businesses with company contacts and social-media profiles.

The buyer is therefore not purchasing a raw page scraper. The product packages search, geographic coverage, result extraction, normalization, optional enrichment, dataset delivery and continued source maintenance as a reusable data service.

### Customer and buyer use case

Typical commercial uses include local B2B prospecting, market mapping, territory analysis, competitor research, directory building, local SEO research and enrichment of CRM/account lists. The product's Lead generation and Travel categorisation reflects that the same source data can support both prospecting and wider business/location intelligence.

For lead generation, the essential workflow is to turn a target definition such as industry plus geography into a structured list of organisations with sufficient identity and contact context for qualification or downstream enrichment.

### Value proposition

The main value is breadth and reliability at scale. A buyer can query thousands of locations without maintaining Google-specific extraction logic, geographic search splitting, pagination, deduplication, proxy/network handling, schemas, storage, scheduling or billing infrastructure.

The mature product also offers optional enrichment and analysis beyond the basic place record. That matters commercially because raw Maps data is widely available from competing Actors; the incumbent proposition is increasingly the combination of coverage, reliability, accumulated edge-case handling and additional buyer-ready fields.

### Demand and traction

Current evidence is exceptionally strong:

- approximately **36K monthly active users**;
- approximately **602K total users**;
- approximately **5.1K bookmarks**;
- approximately **1.8K reviews** at **4.7/5**;
- approximately **3.77M public runs in 30 days** in a 9 September 2026 public-API snapshot, of which **95.7% succeeded**.

The run volume implies intensive repeat use. The product has also operated for many years, so its demand is not dependent on a short launch spike.

### Pricing and monetisation

The current Store page advertises pricing **from $1.50 per 1,000 scraped places**, with the effective price varying by the customer's Apify discount tier. The product also charges additional events for richer place details, contact enrichment, reviews, social-profile enrichment and competitor-analysis features.

This creates a layered monetisation model: a relatively low-priced high-volume core dataset plus optional higher-value events. Apify's general pay-per-event economics pay third-party creators 80% of paid-plan event revenue less platform costs, but this Actor is marked **Maintained by Apify** and the commercial arrangement between Apify and Compass is not public. Customer event spend can therefore be estimated more defensibly than Compass's own payout.

### Revenue estimate

Exact revenue is private. The following estimate is deliberately framed as **customer event spend generated by the Actor**, not reported seller revenue.

#### Observed inputs

- Core pricing from **$1.50 per 1,000 places**, with higher prices on lower discount tiers.
- Optional enrichment/review events add further spend and are excluded from the core estimate below.

#### Central estimate and confidence

A reasonable central order-of-magnitude estimate is **roughly $100K-$250K/month of customer event spend** on the core Actor, before optional enrichment. **Confidence: Low-Medium.** Run count and current pricing are observable; results per run and price-tier mix are inferred. This must not be interpreted as reported Compass revenue or profit.

### Competition and differentiation

Competition is extremely strong. Searching Google Maps scraping on Apify returns many alternatives, including low-cost community products and specialised lead-generation variants. The incumbent remains differentiated by its scale, review history, broad configuration surface, mature reliability, large installed user base and continued adaptation to edge cases.

This case therefore demonstrates both sides of the area: enormous proven demand and a powerful incumbent advantage. A new entrant is unlikely to win by duplicating the basic Maps-to-table function alone.

### Economics and cost drivers

The business model benefits from high software leverage: the same Actor can serve thousands of users and Apify provides execution, datasets, billing, API access and marketplace distribution. Variable cost scales with compute, network traffic, storage, proxy use, retries and optional enrichment work. Review scraping and social/contact enrichment can be significantly more resource-intensive than returning the core place record.

Under standard third-party PPE terms, 80% of paid event revenue is available before platform costs. This does not establish Compass's private economics, but it shows why implementation efficiency matters: at $1.50/1K places, the standard gross creator envelope would be $1.20/1K before platform costs, whereas richer add-ons create more revenue headroom for extra processing.

### Operating model

Commercial delivery is self-service and largely automated through Apify. The operational burden sits in maintaining source coverage and predictable semantics. Public issues include zero/partial results, geographic leakage, runs that fail to terminate as expected and questions about charges versus returned results. Google also exposes practical result limits that require geographic subdivision and deduplication for broader searches.

The case is therefore a low-touch sales/delivery model backed by a high-volume external-source integration that requires continuous reliability work.

## 3. Capability Assessment

| Dimension | Assessment | Evidence / basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical complexity | **Medium-High** | Basic place extraction is straightforward, but mature coverage requires geographic search expansion, pagination, deduplication, enrichment, reviews and extensive edge-case handling. | High |
| Domain expertise | **Medium** | Requires strong knowledge of Google Maps search/location semantics and buyer requirements for business identity, contact and local-market data; specialist industry expertise is not generally required. | High |
| Data / resource access | **Low-Medium** | Core data is publicly visible and Apify supplies the managed platform; reliable large-scale collection still depends on network/proxy resources and continued access to Google Maps. | High |
| Operating complexity | **High** | The external source, very large user base, geographic edge cases and multiple enrichment modes create recurring reliability and support work. | High |
| Cost intensity | **Low-Medium, scale-sensitive** | No proprietary dataset is required, but compute/network/proxy/review/enrichment costs scale with output and can materially affect margin at low per-place prices. | Medium |

### Technical complexity

A narrow Google Maps extractor can be built with moderate engineering effort, but this case represents a mature production product rather than the technical minimum. Commercial-scale functionality includes location/category search semantics, geographic expansion beyond individual-query result limits, place-detail extraction, deduplication, reviews, optional contact/social enrichment, filtering and stable schemas.

The key complexity is preserving completeness and predictability as Google changes source behaviour. At incumbent scale, small failure rates or search-semantic errors affect large numbers of runs and customers, so observability, retry behaviour and backward-compatible outputs become part of the product capability.

### Domain expertise

The required expertise is primarily source and data-product knowledge. A provider needs to understand how Google Maps represents businesses, categories, geographies and result sets, and how buyers interpret fields such as place IDs, categories, addresses, websites, ratings and review counts.

Lead-generation knowledge also matters because useful output is not simply whatever Google exposes. Buyers need deduplicable business identity, geographic targeting and fields that can feed qualification or enrichment workflows. This is meaningful domain knowledge but does not require scarce professional credentials.

### Data / resource access

No proprietary source dataset is evident. The value is produced by transforming publicly exposed Google Maps data and optional public business/contact surfaces. Apify supplies runtime, storage, API access, schedules, billing and distribution.

The remaining dependency is reliable access to Google Maps and any enrichment targets. Network/proxy resources and source-specific request strategies are operational inputs, and the seller has no control over future source-side restrictions or schema changes.

### Operating complexity

Operating complexity is high for the mature benchmark. Public issues show recurring source and search-behaviour problems, and the product supports a wide range of configurations that can fail in different ways. The provider must distinguish true absence of data from search/filter problems, source blocking, geography effects and implementation defects.

The scale also raises the cost of regressions. The commercial interface is automated, but source monitoring, incident response, support and compatibility maintenance are persistent requirements.

### Cost intensity

The case has low fixed infrastructure requirements because Apify supplies the platform and there is no visible proprietary data licence. Costs are primarily variable.

#### Observed cost inputs

- Core customer price starts at **$1.50/1K places**.
- Higher-value events separately charge for reviews, social/contact enrichment and analysis.
- Standard Apify PPE economics for third-party creators are 80% of paid-plan event revenue minus platform costs, although this Actor's Apify/Compass arrangement is private.

#### Cost assumptions

Exact per-place compute, proxy and enrichment consumption is private. For economic sensitivity only, a mature efficient scraper can be modelled with direct platform/resource costs consuming roughly **15%-50% of the standard 80% gross creator envelope** on the core event, with review/enrichment modes potentially higher. This is an analytical assumption, not reported cost.

#### Estimated cost range / scenarios

At a $1.50/1K customer price, the standard third-party gross envelope would be $1.20/1K before costs. A 15%-50% resource-cost share of that envelope implies approximately **$0.18-$0.60/1K** of platform/resource cost and **$0.60-$1.02/1K** remaining contribution before development/support labour. The actual Compass economics may differ materially.

#### Margin / economic impact and confidence

The case can support attractive software margins if extraction remains efficient because customer charging scales directly with useful output. However, very low unit pricing makes unnecessary retries, expensive proxies or review/enrichment requests economically important. **Confidence: Low** for numeric cost estimates; **High** that efficiency and source stability are material margin drivers.

## 4. Case Findings

### Intrinsic characteristics

Google Maps business data has very large recurring demand, highly structured buyer use cases and strong production leverage. The source can support lead generation without proprietary data ownership, but broad geographic completeness and reliable extraction create continuing technical work.

### Case-specific characteristics

The extreme user/run scale, very large review base, wide add-on surface and long history are incumbent characteristics rather than minimum requirements for participation. A smaller product can deliberately target a narrower geography, vertical or lead workflow.

### Wider opportunity-area relevance

The case shows why raw extraction is increasingly commoditised in lead generation. Buyers pay for usable records, but a mature market rewards reliability, completeness, identity/deduplication and enrichment. It also demonstrates that a low unit price can coexist with substantial revenue when usage volume is very high.

### Key uncertainties

- Results per run and paid/free usage mix are private, so revenue estimates are order-of-magnitude only.
- Compass's commercial relationship with Apify is not public; standard creator payout terms should not be assumed to equal Compass payout.
- Search completeness varies by query and geography, so public run success does not prove commercial completeness of every dataset.
- The Store metrics change continuously; values are a 15 September 2026 snapshot.
